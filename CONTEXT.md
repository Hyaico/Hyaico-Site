# HYAICO Company Website — Design Direction

## Intent
HYAICO is a company of innovation; its first product is Just Cooked (justcooked.app). The site must say "innovative, thinks differently, building the future" through how it looks and moves, not through copy. Almost no words. One continuous scroll-driven 3D story that mixes particles with real geometry and makes sense from scene to scene: the Earth in deep space → a dive into it, on into its DNA → the DNA spins into a galaxy → back home at sunset (Just Cooked, where its download links live) → night, the stars align to write HYAICO, which turns to chrome → the ending.

The copy, exactly: "Building *what's next.*" (hero and ending), "Stretching *what can be done.*" (DNA), "And if it's done, *it can be better.*" (galaxy), "For when *food feels hard.*" (Just Cooked). Every title types itself out at a constant pace (time-based, never scrubbed by the scroll): characters fade in smoothly behind a caret that glides along, then blinks softly; on the closing line the caret keeps blinking for good.

Things explicitly rejected in earlier versions and not to return: paragraphs of explanatory copy, "AI company" framing, a bright 3D object behind the hero title, a hero title close to the globe, a plain sphere of particles, a wireframe, grey or stylised dot-grid globe (it is the real Earth: Blue Marble, real countries with borders), particles floating on the globe for no reason (they are the world's city lights), small rings that don't belong on an atom (latitude circles, connection arcs: removed), orbits drawn as round tubes or plain hoops (each is a crisp screen-space line of light in a soft glow, brightening into a trail behind a glinting electron, passing in front of and behind the Earth), a plain dark hero background (it is deep space with the Milky Way), the Earth falling away on the way to the DNA (we dive into it and through its lights), a helix that forms in the centre and slides over, a helix drawn as ribbons in particle fuzz, plastic tubes, or with orbs between segments (it is light: twisted ribbon backbones with a core line and bright edges, tapered base pairs in two blues), a helix that merely dissolves into the galaxy (it spins into it, the way the galaxy turns), a galaxy on flat black, particle effects on the chrome HYAICO, particles writing HYAICO (the stars align to write it), a small, washed-out, hazy or pixelated phone, circles or rings of light, dust clouds, starfields, light columns or a grid floor around the phone, lens ghosts, starbursts or anamorphic streaks at the sun (the sun's light is one soft warm bloom), a big hard-edged or fake-looking sun (it is a small soft disc with a hot heart and a wide glow), an azure that looks too bright or fake (it is a reddish sunset like the reference the user gave: violet-blue overhead, magenta and pink low, coral by the sun, billowing coral and salmon clouds with violet gaps, a purple haze on the horizon, a navy-purple sea; no rocks), a flat or dusky Just Cooked backdrop, still clouds (they drift, in perspective), clouds that read as haze or streaks, any line or band where the sea meets the sky (the sky is a screen-space quad that no clipping can cut), a sea lighter or darker than the sky while the stage fades (the sea is drawn first and mirrors the whole sky; the sky lies on the far plane and only fills what the sea leaves), a sun glint that reads as a spot on the ocean, bloom that blacks out the scene backgrounds (it adds light, never coverage), a galaxy light that jumps to the sun (it glides on one arc to the exact spot, reddening), a muddy brown fade into the sunset (the stage arrives as night with stars and warms as the sun rises), the sun hidden behind the phone, a phone that rises edge-on, copy that is hard to read over the sky (it sits on a pane of dark glass), copy hugging the far left of the phone beat, eyebrows or blinking dots on any beat, screen-indicator bars, a flat or blown-out chrome, plain black backgrounds (each scene has its own background), any fixed wash that reads as a horizontal split or a two-tone background, soft blurry "highlight" reveals in the loader (it is a white laser scan), a second highlight in the loader (no light sweep or glow pulse after the laser), a loader on a separate background from the hero (it shows the hero's own space), a hard rectangle behind the loader logo (never a filter and a mask on one element), a loader counter that changes width, any burst, flash or explosion on hand-over, a Just Cooked button in the nav, a coloured-tile favicon, an invented contact email or roadmap teasers, a trademark line in the footer, a phone that lingers behind the ending, a product CTA as the final frame, a particle version of the wordmark as the ending, an eyebrow or small logo above the closing line, a closing line too high above the wordmark, a closing caret that disappears, characters that pop in or a caret that blinks in hard steps, the typed "Just Cooked" name instead of the logotype, the tagline "Home cooking, made easy", a laggy or offset cursor, anything that hitches the first time it appears, and asset links without a cache-busting query.

## Visual system
- **Theme:** near-black base (`#06060a`) with a background per scene behind the (see-through) canvas (deep space with a blue glow under the Earth; a blue glow behind the DNA; violet, rose and blue nebula colour round the galaxy; deep night blue for the stars and the chrome; a soft violet light under the ending), cross-faded by opacity, and a painted Milky Way with sharp stars in front of them wherever we are in space. Film grain at very low opacity. The Just Cooked beat is the one warm moment: a reddish sunset.
- **Type:** Geist (headlines, weight 500, tight tracking) with Instrument Serif italic for the accent words, Geist Mono for the few labels. Keep this pairing.
- **Accents:** violet `#7c5cff`, cyan `#45e3ff`, Just Cooked orange `#e94b1f`, warm `#ff8a5c`, cream `#fbf6ec` (from the Just Cooked site's tokens).
- **Logos:** `HyaicoWhite.svg` everywhere for HYAICO (nav, loader, constellation, chrome, ending); `JustCookedLogo.svg` (the rebranded logotype) in the phone beat. Favicon: the white H from the wordmark on a transparent background.

## Structure (single page)
1. **Loader** — once the 3D scene has drawn its first frame the veil lifts onto the hero's own deep space; over it the wordmark in polished chrome (a horizon-banded metal gradient masked by the logo, the same metal it becomes at the end), scanned in by a white laser with an anamorphic flare — the only highlight; a counter line with a fixed-width counter. At the hand-over the wordmark glides up into the nav and turns white as it lands, while the Earth rises and the title types itself. Nothing bursts.
2. **Pinned story** (900% scroll):
   - **The Earth** (radius 2.6, centred just below the bottom edge; smaller and higher on phones; Europe and Africa face the viewer first and it turns slowly eastward): NASA Blue Marble by day lit from the upper left, Black Marble city lights on the night side to the right, Natural Earth borders (and faint coasts), a warm terminator band, a broad faint sheen of sunlight on the oceans (never a spot), a cloud shell, an atmosphere at the limb and an outer glow; deep space and the Milky Way behind. The particles are placed by the city-light map, gold, brightest on the night side. The atom: three orbits (1.42× the radius), 60° apart, oriented so each passes in front of the Earth on one side and behind it on the other; each a crisp screen-space line about 1.5 px wide in a soft glow, colour drifting cyan↔violet, brightening into a trail behind its electron (a bright point with a thin horizontal glint); far halves dimmer. They come up with the Earth as it rises. Title above, typed after the loader.
   - **Into the DNA**: one flight forward along a straight line from the hero camera through the face of the Earth to the helix framing (the hero is placed so this line exists): the orbits sweep past and fade, the stars and the Milky Way fall away, the surface melts away just before the camera reaches it, the city lights stream on ahead and weave the helix (two twisted ribbons with a bright centre line and edges, tapered base pairs in light and deep blue with a gap where each pair meets, pulses up the strands, the far side dimmer), which grows from the bottom; the gaze turns from the Earth to the line of flight to the helix. Right of the title on desktop with a slight lean; below it and smaller on phones.
   - **Out to the galaxy**: the helix spins up — one full turn, the way the galaxy turns — while every light swings round the shared axis from its place on the helix to its place in an arm, the helix's height settling into the disc, its lean straightening and its axis gliding to the centre; the ribbon helix spins with them, flattens and fades; the camera pulls far back and up. Warm core, blue-white arms, rose nebulae, stars, flares, nebula haze; the title spans the top.
   - **Back home**: the galaxy dissolves into the night's stars while its core's light glides down one arc on screen to the exact spot on the horizon where the sun sits, warming from white-gold to red-orange; the sky's glow gathers there and the disc takes over where the light lands. The stage arrives in night colours with stars and warms into the sunset as the sun rises to sit half-set on the horizon. The sky is a screen-space quad computing each pixel's view ray: violet-blue zenith, violet, magenta and pink low down, coral on the sun's side, a purple haze on the horizon, the sun (a small soft disc with a hot heart and a wide glow), two layers of billowing clouds from a panorama painted once with seamless periodic noise (heights on a log scale so they keep their shape toward the horizon), lit coral and salmon toward the sun and violet in their own shade, drifted per row in proportion to height (perspective), night colours and procedural stars by a night factor. Glowing motes drift in the air. The sea is the `Water` addon (navy-purple, coral glitter) to the true horizon. The only glare is a soft warm bloom round the sun. The phone rises out of the water facing us. The copy sits on a pane of dark glass (blurred backdrop, soft border) in from the left: logotype, the typed tagline in warm orange italics, cream App Store and glass Google Play buttons, justcooked.app; on phones the pane sits at the bottom over a deepening dusk.
   - **Night falls**: the sky deepens, the sun sinks below the sea, stars come out, the phone sinks, the copy leaves; the stage fades into deep space. About 300 stars (210 on phones), spaced evenly along the outlines of the letters, drift in from all over the sky on gentle arcs and align from the left as if writing the word; faint lines draw from star to star round each letter like a constellation; then the chrome (horizon studio) arrives from the left behind a soft edge, with the travelling light riding that edge and each star flaring once as it passes and goes. The particles are not drawn from here on.
   Only HUD: a bottom-centre scroll cue that hides on first scroll.
3. **Ending** — the chrome glides down and becomes the faint masked wordmark (even tone, bottom fade, light sweep, capped at 1400px); above it, a little below the centre of the free space, "Building *what's next*." types itself out and then draws its violet-to-cyan underline, its caret blinking on for good; a soft violet light behind it; a bar with © HYAICO and back to top.

## Motion
- Scroll scrubs the 3D story and the beats' exits; titles type themselves out on their own clock when their beat is reached (and again if you scroll back): each character fades in over its own slot while the caret glides along, then the caret blinks softly (ease-in-out) a few times and fades away — except on the closing line.
- Forms blend with purpose: the camera dives into the Earth and its lights become the DNA, the DNA spins into the galaxy, the galaxy's light becomes the sun, the stars write HYAICO and it turns to metal; the camera dives in, pulls back, comes down to the sea.
- Particles carry individual parallax depth under the pointer (the city lights stay put); the camera moves only slightly and stops moving as the ending lands. The phone follows the pointer with the Just Cooked site's resting tilt.
- Cursor: dot pinned to the pointer, ring follows tightly, crosshair centred.
- Respects `prefers-reduced-motion` (loader skipped, no typing, forms still follow scroll).

## Performance
- Particles: 60k desktop / 28k phones; helix, galaxy and dust forms built after the first frame; city-light positions sampled once from the night map; not drawn from the sunset on. Bloom only on fine-pointer devices, only on the particle layer, through a 4× multisampled target at 1×. The sea's reflection renders at 1024×512 (384×768 on phones). Phones get the 2k day map and 1k clouds.
- Warm-up under the loader: `compileAsync` for the particle scene (composer target) and the product scene (screen and a reflection-like target), `initTexture` for every texture as it loads, the chrome and the constellation compiled as soon as they exist; the later text painted once, invisibly, during the hand-over.
- The Milky Way (1280² desktop, 768² phones) and the cloud panorama (2048×768 desktop, 1024×640 phones) are painted once, a strip per frame over the first frames; the clouds are repainted only if the view needs a wider period or the sun moves; the sky does two lookups.
- The constellation is ~300 points and ~300 line segments whose motion is computed on the GPU. Nothing is sampled from the logo at load.
- The particle layer is skipped while the sky fully covers it and after the ending; transparent sprites, stars, the orbits and the Earth's surface are culled when unseen. Scene backgrounds are CSS layers animated by opacity only.
- Scroll-scrubbed DOM motion is opacity and transforms only. Minified three.js, modulepreload, trimmed fonts. Pixel ratio capped at 1.5. Rendering pauses when the tab is hidden.
- No build step; pinned CDN versions.

## Constraints
- Static files only, deployable to GitHub Pages; relative asset paths; serve over HTTP locally (ES modules).
